Molecule–Protein Interaction (MPI) prediction

Predicting which molecules bind which proteins underpins drug discovery and molecular function annotation. Two obstacles limit current models:

① Label scarcity

Known molecule–protein pairs cover only a tiny fraction of biologically relevant interactions — supervised models are starved of positives.

② Ignored biological context

Most methods use only molecular/protein features and discard the rich context — genes, pathways, functional annotations — that hints at interaction.

Idea: integrate heterogeneous biology into one knowledge graph, and use optimal transport to turn unlabeled pairs into high-quality pseudo-labels.

Contributions

  • Unified biomedical KG. Six data sources (ChEBI, KEGG, Gene Ontology, PFam, ENZYME, DrugBank) integrated into a single graph spanning drugs, proteins, genes, pathways and functional annotations — millions of relations providing complementary context.
  • OT-based pseudo-labeling. Label assignment is cast as a point-set matching problem solved with optimal transport, yielding pseudo-labels consistent with the global distribution of known interactions.
  • State-of-the-art results. Across virtual screening (DUD-E, LIT-PCBA) and KG link prediction, KGOT improves AUROC, early-recognition metrics and generalization to unseen targets.

A · The unified knowledge graph

Six integrated sources
  • ChEBI + UniProt — catalytic activity (molecule↔protein)
  • KEGG — pathways, modules, compounds, orthology
  • Gene Ontology — biological process / component / function
  • PFam — protein family membership
  • ENZYME — enzyme commission relations
  • DrugBank — drug–target interactions
One heterogeneous graph
  • 8 node types: molecules, proteins, genes, GO terms, pathways, families, EC numbers, …
  • ~29 edge types over millions of relations
  • Nodes carry pretrained structural features (molecule & protein encoders)

The graph supplies the biological context that pure feature-based models discard.

B · Interaction score via inverse optimal transport

A scoring MLP fuses molecule and protein embeddings:

$$ S(x,y) = W\big(f(x)\;\oplus\;g(y)\big),\qquad W:[512,256,1]\text{ MLP} $$

Training takes an inverse-OT view: for a batch, the ground-truth interactions define a target transport plan $T_{gt}$; the model’s predicted plan $T_{pred}$ (from cost $C_{pred}=1-S$) is aligned to it via a KL objective.

$$ \mathcal{L}_{score} = \mathrm{KL}\!\left(T_{pred}\,\|\,T_{gt}\right) $$

This teaches the score to reproduce the transport structure of real interactions, not just point-wise labels.

C · OT pseudo-labeling of unlabeled pairs

Over the large pool of unlabeled molecule–protein pairs, define cost $C_{ij}=1-S_{ij}$ and solve a regularized OT problem for a soft assignment plan $T\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}$:

$$ \min_{T\in\Pi(r,c)}\ \langle T,C\rangle \;-\; \varepsilon\,H(T)\;+\;\lambda\,\Omega_{sim}(T) $$
  • Sinkhorn–Knopp iterations solve the entropic OT efficiently.
  • Similarity constraint $\Omega_{sim}$ encourages similar molecules to receive consistent pseudo-labels.
  • Entries above threshold $\delta$ become high-confidence pseudo-interactions.

Result: a principled way to mine likely interactions that respect the global distribution of known ones.

KGOT · ICLR 2026

D · KG augmentation & multi-objective link prediction

The pseudo-label matrix $T$ is injected into the KG as a new relation type — pseudo-interaction — alongside all observed edges. A KG embedding model is then trained with a joint objective:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{KG} \;+\; \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{pseudo} $$

balancing graph structure with data-driven pseudo-supervision. The framework is model-agnostic — evaluated with PairRE, RotatE, MuRE, TorusE and ComplEx-FF.

“Filling in” likely-but-unobserved interactions yields a more complete graph and better retrieval of held-out links.

Takeaways

  • Context matters. Unifying molecular, protein, gene and pathway data into one KG injects biological signal that feature-only models miss.
  • OT pseudo-labeling works. Casting label assignment as optimal transport turns abundant unlabeled pairs into consistent supervision — model-agnostic and consistently beneficial.
  • Strong, general results. New state of the art on DUD-E and LIT-PCBA virtual screening, and consistent link-prediction gains across five KG embedding models.
  • Scalable & label-efficient. A practical route to MPI prediction when labeled interactions are scarce.

Knowledge Graph + Optimal Transport = label-efficient molecule–protein interaction prediction.
